The DIRA Group

The DIRA Group comprises DIRA, its parent technology company, and the ventures it has established to extend its work into new sectors and audiences. Each venture operates independently and serves a distinct purpose, while drawing on the same technical team and the same operating standard. This page provides an overview of each.

DIRA Parent technology company
  1. Property Management Software

    DHUB

    DHUB is Cameroon's premier property-management and booking software as a service (SaaS). Renters can browse single rooms, studios, apartments, hotels, and guest houses. Individual landlords and owners of multi-tenant properties can manage properties; university hostels can manage accommodation, while hotels and guest houses can manage bookings.

    DHUB serves individual renters and landlords, property businesses, and institutional property operators. It is available on Google Play and the web.

  2. Philanthropy & Community

    DIRA Foundation

    DIRA Foundation is the philanthropic and community-facing arm of the DIRA Group. It supports community initiatives and extends technical resources to nonprofit organisations, community associations, and alumni networks that may not otherwise have access to them. This work is distinct from DIRA SAS, which also serves NGOs and nonprofits as paying clients.

    Through DIRA Foundation, DIRA has delivered web development, cybersecurity support, and digital infrastructure to a number of community and nonprofit organisations, applying the same standards of quality and delivery used in its commercial engagements. DIRA Foundation reflects the company's position that technical capability, once developed, should also serve the community in which the company operates.

  3. Annual Flagship Festival

    DIRA Tech Fest

    DIRA Tech Fest is the DIRA Group's annual technology festival, held in Bamenda. The festival combines structured technical training, product development, and public programming over a three-day period, and has become the Group's most visible public initiative to date.

    The inaugural 2026 edition trained over one hundred participants across ten technical tracks, supported ten teams through a forty-eight-hour product development sprint, and convened representatives of three government ministries, a regional financial institution, and industry participants for a public discussion on the digital economy of the Northwest Region. The festival also served as the public launch platform for DHUB. Subsequent editions are planned on an expanded scale.

  4. Commercial Production Arm

    DIRA Content Studio

    DIRA Content Studio is a production arm of DIRA and a distinct revenue line for the company, rather than a venture of the wider Group. It provides video production, photography, podcast recording, and branded content services to businesses, institutions, and independent creators.

    The Studio also produces all media published across the DIRA Group, and is developing a regional technology and business reporting function covering the digital economy of the Northwest Region.
