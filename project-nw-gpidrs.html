Regional Digital Readiness Programme

Northwest Government Digital Readiness Survey

The Northwest Government and Public Institutions Digital Readiness Survey (NW-GPIDRS) is a proposed field-assessment programme to build a clearer, evidence-based picture of digital capacity across public institutions in Cameroon's North West Region.

Programme focus

Public institutions

Geographic scope

North West Region, all eight divisions

Assessment scope

20–35 institutions; planned over 6–8 months

Institutional context

Prepared for the Regional Delegation of MINPOSTEL

Why a regional baseline matters

Public offices rely on dependable records, communications and services to support residents and coordinate administration. Yet the availability of computers, reliable connectivity, secure backups, digital records and staff with relevant skills can vary considerably between institutions.

Without consistent, institution-level evidence, regional planning may not reflect the practical conditions in individual offices. NW-GPIDRS is designed to map those conditions through structured visits, helping decision-makers understand where the region is well equipped and where foundational gaps need attention.

The programme is intended to complement the parallel Northwest Digital Readiness Survey for businesses and social-economy organisations. Together, the two assessment tracks are designed to inform a broader view of the region's public- and private-sector digital ecosystem.

A structured, institution-first assessment

The proposed programme covers regional ministerial delegations, divisional and sub-divisional offices, selected local councils and, where access is authorised, regional administration offices. Each participating institution would receive a structured on-site assessment and a profile of its current readiness.

  • Connectivity and equipmentAvailability and condition of computers, internet access, power and local networking.
  • Data and records managementHow official registers and files are stored, backed up, retrieved and moved from paper-based processes.
  • Digital public servicesOnline service delivery, digital access points and readiness to provide citizen-facing services.
  • Inter-institutional communicationUse of official communications and ability to share information with other offices and relevant platforms.
  • Staff digital capacityStaff computer skills, prior training and access to dedicated IT or systems support.
  • Governance and procurement readinessTechnology planning, procurement processes and institutional capacity to sustain digital improvements.

From field visits to useful insight

The concept uses a consistent assessment framework, with each domain scored to support both an individual institutional profile and a comparable regional view. The intent is to identify practical priorities—not to assume that every institution needs the same technology or level of digitisation.

Planned outputs include tailored institutional feedback, progress updates, an aggregated Northwest Government Digital Readiness Index and a final regional report with policy recommendations. A combined public- and private-sector ecosystem overview is also envisaged alongside the parallel NWDRS programme.

Individual institutional findings are not presented on this page. Any public-facing regional insight is intended to use aggregated information rather than disclose an institution's private assessment.

Regional and national relevance

A field-based regional baseline can help make digital transformation plans more responsive to actual operating conditions. The programme is framed to support Cameroon’s National Development Strategy 2020–2030 (SND30) and provide locally grounded evidence relevant to public-sector digital transformation and PATNUC planning.

By documenting needs across the region's eight divisions, the assessment framework is intended to support better-targeted infrastructure, staff-capacity and digital-service interventions, and to offer a method that can be adapted for use in other regions.
