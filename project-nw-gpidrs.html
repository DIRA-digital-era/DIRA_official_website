Regional Digital Readiness Programme

Northwest Government Digital Readiness Survey

Through the Northwest Government and Public Institutions Digital Readiness Survey (NW-GPIDRS), DIRA is assessing digital capacity across public institutions in Cameroon's North West Region and building a practical, evidence-based picture of regional needs.

Programme focus

Public institutions

Geographic scope

North West Region, all seven divisions

Assessment target

20–35 institutions over an estimated 6–8 months

Institutional context

Prepared for the Regional Delegation of MINPOSTEL

Indicative programme value

30–50 million XAF, funded entirely by DIRA

Programme budget and financing

The programme's estimated value is 30,000,000–50,000,000 XAF. DIRA covers the implementation costs; MINPOSTEL and the Government of Cameroon are not asked to contribute financially.

Why a regional baseline matters

Public offices rely on dependable records, communications and services to support residents and coordinate administration. Yet the availability of computers, reliable connectivity, secure backups, digital records and staff with relevant skills can vary considerably between institutions.

Without consistent, institution-level evidence, regional planning may not reflect the practical conditions in individual offices. Through structured field visits, NW-GPIDRS is documenting those conditions to help decision-makers identify existing strengths and foundational gaps.

Alongside the Northwest Digital Readiness Survey for businesses and social-economy organisations, NW-GPIDRS is building a broader picture of the region's public- and private-sector digital ecosystem.

A structured, institution-first assessment

The programme covers regional ministerial delegations, divisional and sub-divisional offices, selected local councils and, where access is authorised, regional administration offices. Participating institutions receive a structured on-site assessment and a profile of their current readiness.

  • Connectivity and equipmentAvailability and condition of computers, internet access, power and local networking.
  • Data and records managementHow official registers and files are stored, backed up, retrieved and moved from paper-based processes.
  • Digital public servicesOnline service delivery, digital access points and readiness to provide citizen-facing services.
  • Inter-institutional communicationUse of official communications and ability to share information with other offices and relevant platforms.
  • Staff digital capacityStaff computer skills, prior training and access to dedicated IT or systems support.
  • Governance and procurement readinessTechnology planning, procurement processes and institutional capacity to sustain digital improvements.

From field visits to useful insight

The programme uses a consistent assessment framework, with each domain scored to support both an individual institutional profile and a comparable regional view. The findings identify practical priorities rather than assume that every institution needs the same technology or level of digitisation.

DIRA is preparing tailored institutional feedback and progress updates, as well as an aggregated Northwest Government Digital Readiness Index and a regional report with policy recommendations. The two assessment tracks are also informing a combined public- and private-sector ecosystem overview.

Individual institutional findings are not presented on this page. Any public-facing regional insight is intended to use aggregated information rather than disclose an institution's private assessment.

Regional and national relevance

A field-based regional baseline helps make digital transformation plans more responsive to actual operating conditions. The programme supports Cameroon’s National Development Strategy 2020–2030 (SND30) and provides locally grounded evidence relevant to public-sector digital transformation and PATNUC planning.

By documenting needs across the region's seven divisions, the assessment supports better-targeted infrastructure, staff-capacity and digital-service interventions, and provides a method that can be adapted for use in other regions.
